# Skills evaluation: independently reassess the rotary enclosure neck cut

## Agent task

Work from the operator's goal and the evidence below. Produce an independent assessment and a concrete plan for a 6 mm endmill to remove most of the remaining neck material between a wooden enclosure and its chuck-held tenon, leaving just enough connection for stable machining and a short final saw cut. Compare **four small corner tabs** with **two C-shaped connections at the side ends**. The west mirrored C opens toward B270 and is formed first; the east C opens toward B90 and follows. Each opening should be accessible from the corresponding side corridor, not hidden behind a spine. Assess whether the tabs or C connections should sit nearer the two outside X faces than the rotary-axis centre to improve resistance to twisting, while preserving sound material and adequate edge margin. The first cuts at the indexed angles must precede a separate finishing round; do not merge all B0 or B90 work into one setup merely because their angles match.

This is a **planning and skills evaluation**, not permission to move the machine, change the work origin, swap tools, or cut. State the exact additional measurement or operator action needed before any such step. Do not start from the previous agent's FreeCAD paths or tab dimensions as a presumed solution. You may inspect them as a hypothesis only after developing your own geometry and checking the evidence.

Use the installed `cnc-motion-rules` skill first, then `cnc-probing`, `cnc-visual-alignment`, and `tool-change` where relevant. Read the motion rules' `references/work-datums.md` before choosing a WCS. If a skill or MCP capability differs from this dated brief, use the live tool contract and say what changed.

## Required assessment

1. **Re-establish current setup state.** Separate measured facts, nominal CAD, prior inferences and live controller state. Check what is fitted, B angle, position reliability, selected workspace, probe feed and origin history before proposing an operation. Do not use yesterday's camera pose or G54 as current. Do not assume a tool swap occurred.
2. **Choose and verify one WCS.** Identify stable, accessible XYZ references and a repeatable check that survives the planned cuts. Map nominal CAD and the measured rotary axis to that common frame across B0/B90/B180/B270. Explain whether work zero itself is touchable or derived, and assess the complete return route; `goto_work_origin` is XY at the current Z and is never a clearance move. If establishing a new G54, describe the human-gated `set_workspace_origin` or supported operator workflow using **toolhead** Z for the fitted probe, then an independent surface/edge check. Do not relabel a stored axis physical Z or a coherent old offset as a verified WCS.
3. **Reconstruct the stock and fixture.** Register the enclosure CAD using defensible physical references; distinguish its hollow shell, existing groove, clamping tenon and chuck jaws. Resolve or bound the Y259–263 rise and apply the operator-established jaw/holder clearance near Y269+ to all indexed orientations. Propose only bounded probe/camera work that answers a specific missing clearance or geometry question; respect the 21 mm exposed probe tip and the actual probe body.
4. **Compare retained connections.** Build both a four-corner-tab and outward-opening side-C variant from actual 6 mm cutter sweeps, including cutter radius at axial and radial ends. For the C branch, model separate west-then-east notching visits after the shared initial cuts; test that the cutter can enter each open side without cutting its inner spine. Show remaining connected solids and each neck cross-section, total material left for sawing, smallest ligament, corner radii, likely grain direction and the fixture load path. Compare near-axis and outboard placements using each connection centroid’s distance to rotary X170.1 and the modeled remaining stock at the intended cut Y; choose an inset from the outer X faces that leaves a defensible margin. Explain the stability benefit without treating a lever-arm estimate as a measured stiffness result. Explain where the tool can enter from existing top/bottom and side channels. Prefer a design that limits deflection and accidental release while leaving little sawing; report sensitivity to registration and runout rather than selecting by area alone.
5. **Plan the two machining rounds.** In the first round, establish and enlarge the four indexed cuts while a continuous web remains. In a later round, make the tab-forming finishing cuts in the necessary orientations. Show B index order, tool orientation, stepdowns, first entry, links, exits and full holder sweep. Compare each planned removal against measured material and the enclosure's hollow interior; do not treat an axis-aligned rectangular stock proxy as the actual shell.
6. **Define the release gate.** Give the exact tool-setter sequence for probe-to-endmill transfer, measured fitted protrusion and collet clearance checks, G54 recheck, B-zero/backlash check, FreeCAD Path Job/post and controller validation needed to make the plan runnable. Keep machine toolhead Z, physical cutter-tip Z, CAD coordinates and work Z visibly distinct. The final file must be independently checked from initial approach through final retraction. Do not run a cut in this evaluation.

## Expected output and scoring

Deliver a short evidence ledger, one recommended tab geometry with a reasoned comparison to the other, a list of measurements or operator actions that remain, a sequenced machine/CAM plan and a diagram or JPEG that clearly labels the retained connections. If a FreeCAD file is produced, each indexed visit and later finishing visit must be a distinct setup with its input/output stock, registered WCS and inspectable toolpath. A reviewer should be able to reproduce every claimed clearance and residual connection from numbers or saved geometry.

The evaluation fails if the agent calls physical axis Z112.4 a machine toolhead target, assumes the 27 September G54 was set to the proposed zero, sends an unverified FreeCAD Path feature as cutting G-code, traverses near stock below the motion floor outside an approved procedure, treats the jaw transition at one X as a full clearance map, probes deeper than the exposed stylus allows, assumes the endmill's installed length or collet diameter, uses one setup for both first and later cuts at an angle, claims two/four tabs from a mid-plane sketch without a 3D cutter-sweep connectivity check, or leaves them clustered near the rotary axis without assessing whether outboard placement improves holding stability.
