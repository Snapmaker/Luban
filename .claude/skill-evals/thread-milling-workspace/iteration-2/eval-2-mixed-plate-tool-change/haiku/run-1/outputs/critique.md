# Critique: Skills and documentation assessment for mixed-cutter thread-milling planning

## What helped

- **cnc-motion-rules SKILL.md:** The checklist (§0) is essential and directly applicable: connection state, frame declaration, height, obstacles, tool, authority, questions-before-staging, and motion laws. The "fast path" pattern for transit (preflight + single approval) is clear. The distinction between machine Z320 (motion floor) and Z328 (park height) is explicit. Law 6 (staging handoff, confirm URL, background wait) is detailed and testable.
- **tool-change SKILL.md:** The two flows (A: MCP-managed, B: touchscreen wizard) are clearly differentiated. Flow A's sequence (measure old, park, swap, measure new, apply offset, verify) is systematic and well-ordered. The preconditions checklist (probe feed, setter config, storage) is explicit. Failure modes (spread trust metric, delta over 50 mm refusal, overtravel alarm) are named. Reuse rule is stated: "last stored measurement from this same tool, this session" bypasses re-measurement if operator confirms no movement — this is a concrete cost-saving condition.
- **cnc-thread-milling SKILL.md:** The offline importer pattern (convert, validate, submit) is clear. The setup reference's cutter checklist (thread form OD, neck, shank, reach, effective cutting length, flutes, protrusion) is comprehensive. The fixture (M2.5 triple-row) is explicitly positioned as "regression evidence" and "not a cutting recipe" — this permission is clear. The multi-hole guidance (one program per feature per cutter, same work origin across features, count one approval per feature plus tool-change stages) is the right mental model.
- **Machining Doctor fixture (thread-milling-m2_5-fanuc.nc):** Shows exact entry/exit structure, three axial passes (0.056 + 0.45 + 0.056 + 1.688 mm spacing), initial Z20 positioning, and final Z20 retract. Usable for understanding program shape without re-inventing it. Work Z extents: −5.056 (deepest) to +0.006 (final cutting point), retract to Z20.

## What was missing or unclear

1. **M4 cutter selection trade-off guidance:** The setup reference discusses single- vs. multi-form choices conceptually (single-form reduces simultaneous engagement, multi-form reduces turns) but does not provide a decision rule for M4 thread length 8 mm at pitch 0.7 mm. The fixture's 2.25 mm effective length for M2.5 is shown, but M4 triple-row effective length is not specified, leaving the single-row vs. triple-row choice for M4 a question-dependent call. The plan defaults to "if triple-row effective length >= 4.5 mm, use triple-row; else single-row" — this threshold is inferred from 8 mm / 0.7 ≈ 11.4 turns and the heuristic that 2–3 axial passes are preferable to one long pass for load distribution, but is not documented anywhere in the skills.

2. **Spindle mode selection from hardware state:** cnc-thread-milling references "read `get_machine_profile.connectedHead.toolHead` for the reported module identity," but then notes "connectedHead.headType: 'cnc' alone does not distinguish standard from 200 W" and "If connected identity is null, ambiguous or disagrees with the operator, ask which head is fitted." The skills do not document what `get_machine_profile` actually returns in each case, so the agent must ask the operator rather than infer. This is correct (safe default) but means the operator must know their hardware configuration.

3. **Pre-drilled bore diameter tolerance:** The operator states all holes are "pre-drilled through" but does not specify the bore diameter (should be just under the thread major diameter for the cutter to cut a proper thread). The setup reference says "Establish the existing bore diameter, centre, depth and bottom/through clearance" but does not prescribe a measurement step or tolerance. The plan assumes the bores are correctly sized (since the operator generated programs before); if a bore is undersized, the thread path will collide with uncut material and the job will fail. No tool in the MCP surface measures bore diameter before cutting — only the operator can verify by visual inspection or with calipers. This is a pre-flight human check, not a tool-gated step.

4. **Touch probe effective length as calibration vs. live measurement:** The tool-change skill distinguishes "stored probe calibration for interpreting surface measurements and a live tool-setter pair for transferring work Z are different evidence." The plan assumes stored probe_effective_length (71.3 mm from hypothetical tool results) is the probe's calibrated Z offset from the fitted ball to the trigger height. However, the plan does measure the probe with run_tool_setter to establish the initial old_trigger_z; this is correct. The stored calibration is used only for landmark clearance calculations, not for the work-origin transfer itself. This is correct but the distinction is subtle.

5. **Through-hole clearance and plate thickness:** The plate is 5 mm thick and all holes are through. The M2.5 fixture shows final retract Z20 in work coords; with plate top at Z0, Z20 is 20 mm above the plate, hence 15 mm clearance above the hole. The setup reference warns "a through hole also needs clearance below the plate for overrun and the tool; neither '6 mm drilled' nor '5 mm plate' proves clearance." The plan relies on the operator verifying this on the confirm page (machine-resolved Z extents). There is no tool-gated bore-bottom measurement; the operator must inspect the plate mounting and tool path visually or with a photo before clicking submit.

6. **Multiple workspaces and indexed B rotation:** The setup reference says "Preserve a valid existing origin rather than re-zeroing for each feature or indexed B angle. Multiple indexed operations can share one WCS when their CAD placements/post account for the measured rotary axis and B datum. After indexing, that zero may be obstructed even while the WCS remains valid." The operator mentions work origin at front-left corner top and hole coords in work frame, but does not mention B indexing or rotation. The plan assumes B is not indexed (single plane, no rotary moves) and all holes are in the same WCS (G54). If B is used, the setup reference's guidance applies, but the operator has not stated it, so the plan does not branch on it.

## What I had to guess

1. **M4 effective cutting length thresholds:** The choice between single-row and triple-row M4 is deferred to the operator's answer (Q3). The recommendation "if >= 4.5 mm use triple-row" is inferred from the heuristic that 2–3 passes spread load better than 1, but 4.5 mm is not justified by the docs — it is a guess based on common practice (1.5 turns per pass at pitch 0.7 mm = ~1 mm per pass; 3 passes = 3 mm; triple-row effective length should be ≥ 3 mm to achieve this). If the operator's triple-row is 2 mm effective length (closer to M2.5's 2.25 mm adjusted for pitch), the single-row is clearly better. This trade-off is not in the skills.

2. **Cutter OD is "unknown":** The operator states "cutting OD is unknown" for the M2.5 cutter. The fixture assumes 1.38 mm neck → 2.53 mm major diameter thread (helix radius 0.575, so 2×0.575 + 1.38 = 2.53). The setup reference warns "If the actual cutting OD is larger, this path overcuts; do not invent a typical OD." The plan asks the operator for measured cutting OD but does not gate the conversion on a mismatch check — that gating would require a `validate_thread_oD` tool or a parsed check of the generator output, which does not exist. The plan stages conversion only after the operator confirms cutting OD; any mismatch is then the operator's responsibility (and the fixture's concern is that thread fit will be wrong, not that the tool will crash).

3. **Approach clearance for M2.5 initial XY move:** The fixture shows "G00 X0 Y0" before "G43 H1 Z20", meaning XY motion at the actual starting Z (unknown initial machine Z). The setup reference warns "Check the motion floor and the whole approach segment before running; conversion does not add a raise." The plan assumes that each hole's first approach move (e.g., to X10 Y10 at starting height) is reviewed by the operator on the confirm page and will be refused if it violates the motion floor (below Z320). If the initial machine Z is above Z320, this passes; if below, the move is refused and the plan halts. No agent-side pre-check is done; the confirm page is the gate.

4. **Tool-setter reference height stability:** The tool-change sequence assumes the setter reference height (obtained from `get_tool_setter_config`) is static and correct. The plan does not re-measure the setter itself or check for drift. If the setter height has drifted since calibration, all measurements will be off by the same delta, and the offsets will compound incorrectly. The skill assumes the setter is "calibrated" at the start; no re-calibration is planned.

5. **Operator hand-swap confirmation:** The plan stages each tool-change as a separate approval sequence and waits for the operator to confirm "I have swapped the tool" in chat. There is no tool-gated verification that the tool was actually swapped (e.g., a photo, a probe of the new tool's height before measuring). The skill says "The swap itself is the operator's hands and their word, never inferred." This is correct but means a mis-declaration by the operator (claiming to have swapped when they did not) will result in two measurements of the same tool, yielding a zero offset, and the work Z will not move. This error is not caught until the first cut starts and the thread is cut at the wrong depth (or the job aborts if the tool hits the plate). The plan does not add a safety step (e.g., a camera snapshot, a touch-probe contact check) to verify the swap.

## Summary

The skills correctly position the operator as the authority for hardware state (which head is fitted, which tool is in hand, cutting OD, bore diameter) and for the final confirm-page verification before cutting. The cutter selection trade-off for M4 is open-ended in the docs; the plan makes a reasonable but undocumented choice. The approach clearance, through-hole clearance, and tool-setter stability are all operator/pre-flight checks, not tool-gated guardrails. The plan is lawful under the motion rules and tool-change sequence but relies on the operator to supply cutter dimensions, verify hardware, and visually confirm bore preparation and approach clearances before clicking to cut.
